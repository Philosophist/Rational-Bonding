    How pieces move in 3D
    • Rook – straight along any of the 3 axes, any distance. In Classic mode (toggle) also any distance along a flat diagonal, one that changes two coordinates but never all three.
    • Bishop – any diagonal: within a plane, including between levels, or corner to corner through the cube (20 directions).
    • Queen – rook + bishop: any straight line (26 directions).
    • King – one step to any of the 26 neighbouring cells.
    • Castling – the rook moves two squares toward the king along the back rank, and the king jumps to just past it: in line, or one diagonal step off the line (up a rank or a level), if that square is empty. Neither may have moved, the squares between must be empty, and the king can't be in, pass through, or land in check.
    • Knight – 2 along one axis then 1 along another (24 jumps).
    • Pawn – forward is both “up the ranks” and “up the levels” toward the enemy edge. Moves straight 1 (or 2 on its first move) along either, but can't capture straight ahead. Captures one diagonal step that goes forward on at least one of those, e.g. forward a rank while dropping a level, so pawns meeting head-on can take each other. Up-forward (forward a rank and up a level at once) is both forward and diagonal: the pawn can move or capture 1 square that way, or 2 on its first move if the square in between is empty (Black: down-forward). Promotes on the enemy back-rank edge. No en passant.
    • White's back rank sits on the near bottom edge (rank 1, level A) with its pawns one step diagonally in front (rank 2, level B). Black mirrors this on the far top edge (rank 8, level H; pawns on rank 7, level G).

    Relative mode. Each piece faces a direction, and space is relative to it: its own three straight axes are its facing and the two directions square to it. Facing a flat diagonal, those are the file plus the two diagonals of its rank/level plane, so the diagonals become straight lines for it. Both armies start facing each other across the cube's diagonal.

    • Rook – any distance along its own three axes.
    • Pawn – like Classic it has several forwards: straight ahead along its facing, and its own up and down. It moves 1 square along any of them (2 on its first move). From the start, facing the diagonal, its up (a rank back, a level up) and down (a rank on, a level down) reach the edge after one. It captures one diagonal step combining one forward with a sideways (or up/down) step, never backwards. By default it may only face toward the enemy.
    • Bishop (toggle, on) – along the diagonals of its own frame. Square to the board it is the classic bishop. Turned 45°, as at the start, the board's rank and level axes become diagonals for it (two squares at a time) and its corner-to-corner lines run like (1, 2, 0).
    • Knight (toggle, on) – 2 along one of its own axes and 1 along another. Facing the start diagonal, "forward" is a diagonal step and only left/right stays a single board axis, so it leaps like (1, 2, 2), (2, 1, 1) or (0, 1, 3).
    • After moving a piece whose moves depend on its facing, labelled blocks appear around it: click one to face that way (click a straight one again to turn it 45°), then confirm. Queens and kings move as in Classic.
    • A piece can face along an axis or a flat diagonal, not corner to corner, because only those have square axes on the grid. Facing along an axis, it can be square to the board or turned 45° (◇).
