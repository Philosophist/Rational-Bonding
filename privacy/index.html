Privacy

The short version: no ads, no trackers, no selling anything about you. We keep only what it takes to run your account, and you can ask for all of it to be deleted.

Last updated 9 November 2026

Who we are

Rational Bonding is the edutainment project of Steve The Philosophist. This policy covers rationalbonding.com and everything on it, plus ourgument.com, Ourgument's own address: the homepage, the blog, Cube Chess, Ourgument and any future games here.

One account for everything

There is one Rational Bonding account. Sign up once, in any of our games, and the same account and username work across all of them. Signing in on one page of rationalbonding.com signs you in on the others too, because they share one sign-in. Ourgument.com uses the same account, but because it is a different website your browser keeps its sign-in separately, so you sign in there once on its own. Passkeys work only on rationalbonding.com.

If you just visit

  • We set no cookies and run no analytics. There are no ads, no tracking pixels and no "like" buttons from social networks.
  • Like any website, the services that deliver our pages see your IP address and browser type for each request. That's GitHub Pages, which hosts the site, Cloudflare, which delivers ourgument.com, and jsDelivr, which serves a few open-source libraries (such as the 3D engine and the QR-code maker). They keep short-lived logs for security and don't share them with us as personal data.
  • Your browser stores a few settings on your own device, such as the chess rules and clock you last picked. These never leave your device.
  • Signed out, Ourgument maps and the views you give on them are saved in your own browser. They never leave your device unless you download a map file and share it yourself.

If you make an account

Accounts are run by Supabase, which stores our database. Here's what it holds and who can see it.

  • Private to you:
    • how you sign in: your email address, or your phone number, or the account you signed in with (such as Google), or your wallet address
    • a scrambled (hashed) password, if you set one, or a passkey's public key. We never see the password itself.
    • sign-in records, such as when you signed in and from which IP address, kept for security
    • games you save from same-screen play
    • your Ourgument maps, including drafts you haven't posted
    • your Ourgument views (agree, disagree, one of the "Neither" answers including any words you type under "Other", how confident, how relevant), unless you choose to share one, or turn on "Share my views by default"
    • the dated history of your views, such as "agreed on 3 March, disagreed on 9 November". This stays private even for views you share.
    • who wrote a Ourgument post you posted anonymously. The database keeps it, so only you can edit or delete that post, but nobody else can see it.
    • your Ourgument settings
  • Public, by design:
    • your username
    • who you follow
    • online games between accounts: the players, the moves and the result, so they can be watched and replayed
    • Ourgument posts: a statement or arrow becomes public only when you choose to post it. It shows your username unless you tick "Post Anonymously". Once someone else has rated a post, its words can't be changed, so nobody can change what people agreed or disagreed with.
    • Ourgument views you choose to share, with your username
    • Ourgument maps you choose to share ("Share this map publicly"): everything on the map, including statements you haven't posted, with your username, on your profile and in your followers' feeds. Your views on it are left out of the shared copy, and posts you made anonymously aren't linked to it. "Stop sharing this map" removes the shared copy.
    • the totals for each Ourgument post (how many agree, disagree and so on, and the average confidence), without saying who rated what

We use this only to run your account and the games. We don't sell it, rent it or use it for advertising. Database rules make sure nobody else can read your private details or change your games.

Signing in with Google or another provider

If you choose "Sign in with Google" (or GitLab, GitHub and the like), that provider tells us your email address, name and profile picture, and confirms it's really you. We use the email to recognise you next time. We don't get your password or access to anything else in that account. The provider's own privacy policy covers what it records about the sign-in.

Emails and text messages

We only send messages you ask for: sign-in links and codes, sign-up confirmations and password resets. Emails are sent through Brevo, an email delivery service based in France. It handles your address only to deliver that message and keeps a delivery log for a limited time. We don't use its open or click tracking. Text messages for phone sign-in go through a text message service in the same way. There is no newsletter and no marketing email. If one ever starts, it will be opt-in.

Playing by invite link

Guest games by link connect the two browsers directly (WebRTC). A free connection service, the PeerJS server, introduces the two browsers to each other. After that, moves and chat travel straight between the players and aren't stored by us. As with any direct connection, each player's browser can see the other's IP address.

Donations

  • Monero: the address and QR code are shown on the page and nothing is sent to us. Monero payments are private by design, so we can't see who sent them.
  • Cash App: the button takes you to Cash App. The payment and its details are handled by Cash App under its own privacy policy. We see what Cash App shows any recipient, such as your $cashtag or name.
  • Referral links (on More ways) take you to the other company's site. That company may tell us a referral happened, but not who you are.

Who helps run the site

  • GitHub Pages: hosts the website
  • Cloudflare: answers the "where is rationalbonding.com" (DNS) lookups, and delivers the pages of ourgument.com by fetching them from the main site. Traffic to rationalbonding.com itself doesn't pass through it.
  • jsDelivr: serves open-source code libraries
  • Supabase: accounts and the game database
  • Brevo (France): sends sign-in emails
  • PeerJS: connects players in link games
  • Google and other sign-in providers: only if you choose them

Some of these companies are in the United States, so your data may be processed there.

How long we keep things

Your account stays until you ask us to delete it. Deleting an account removes your sign-in details, your profile and your saved games. Online games you started are deleted with it. Online games someone else started stay in their history, with your name removed. Your Ourgument maps, views, view history and settings are deleted. Your Ourgument posts stay, without your name, because other people's arguments may rest on them. You can delete a post yourself first, as long as nobody else has rated it. Sign-in records are cleared automatically by Supabase over time.

Your choices and rights

  • You can play every game as a guest, with no account at all.
  • You can change your username at any time from your profile.
  • You can ask us for a copy of your data, to correct it, or to delete your account. We'll do it within 30 days.
  • Wherever you live, you get the same rights. If your local law (such as the GDPR in Europe or the CCPA in California) gives you more, we'll honour those too.

Children

Our games are fun for all ages, but accounts are for people 13 and older. If you're younger, please play as a guest. If you think a child under 13 has made an account, tell us and we'll delete it.

Contact

Email about anything on this page.

For privacy questions or requests, open an issue on our GitHub page. Issues there are public, so please don't put your email address or other personal details in it. Just ask for a private contact and we'll reply.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it here and change the date at the top. If a change affects how we use account data, we'll post a notice on the site before it takes effect.
